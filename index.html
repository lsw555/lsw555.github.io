Researching media, data & society

Computational methods for the questions that shape public life.

I am Sanguk Lee, Ph.D., an Assistant Professor of Media & Communication at Hankuk University of Foreign Studies. I study how AI, climate change communication, and digital media influence public discourse, belief, and behavior.

Research focus

I use computational social science to understand how people encounter, interpret, and respond to consequential issues—from climate change to emerging AI systems.

01 / Research

At the intersection of data, media, and society.

My work connects large-scale communication data with social-scientific questions about public opinion, technology, and social change.

01

Climate communication

How do climate beliefs form, vary, and change across places and publics?

I examine belief systems, persuasive communication, media coverage, and public opinion to better understand how people make sense of climate change.

Public opinionNews mediaClimate beliefs

02

AI & algorithms

What do AI systems reveal—and reproduce—about public discourse?

I investigate large language models, algorithmic bias, and changing conversations around AI in everyday life.

LLMsAlgorithmic biasAI discourse

03

Networked communication

How do social connections organize information, support, and misinformation?

I use network thinking to study beliefs, online support, digital traces, and the relationships that structure communication.

NetworksDigital tracesSocial support

04 / About

Curious about the systems that shape communication.

My research brings together computational social science, science communication, and the study of emerging media. Before joining HUFS, I was a Postdoctoral Associate at Yale University’s School of the Environment and an Assistant Professor at Texas Christian University.

I earned my Ph.D. in Communication from Michigan State University. I also teach courses on AI for data journalism, climate change communication, data communication, and algorithmic media and society.
