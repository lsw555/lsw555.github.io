Computational social science · Media & communication

AI, climate change communication, and computational social science.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Division of Media & Communication at Hankuk University of Foreign Studies. My research uses computational methods to examine public discourse, belief, and behavior.

Explore research Google Scholar ↗

Research focus

My work brings communication theory and large-scale data together to understand the social consequences of emerging technologies and environmental change.

Research

Three connected areas of inquiry.

I study timely social questions through the lenses of communication, computational analysis, and public life.

01

Climate change communication

How do people form climate beliefs, respond to persuasive communication, and encounter climate change through news and digital media?

02

AI, algorithms, and society

How do large language models and algorithmic systems shape public discourse, bias, and everyday communication?

03

Networked communication

How do networks organize beliefs, misinformation, social support, and communication in online communities?

About

Background

I earned my Ph.D. in Communication from Michigan State University. Before joining HUFS, I was a Postdoctoral Associate at Yale University’s School of the Environment and an Assistant Professor at Texas Christian University.

My research interests include computational social science, large language models, science communication, and digital media.
