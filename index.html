Bio

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Media and Communication at Hankuk University of Foreign Studies (HUFS). My work examines the social implications of artificial intelligence and digital media, combining communication theory with computational approaches.

I welcome students interested in rigorous research, transparent methods, and projects with real-world relevance.

What we value

  • Clarity: research questions that are precise and falsifiable.
  • Transparency: reproducible workflows and careful documentation.
  • Impact: work that speaks to core scholarly debates and societal needs.
  • Mentorship: structured guidance and collaborative problem solving.

Research

Define 3–5 research themes. Each card should be concrete: question, data, method, and what a student would do.

AI, Media, and Public Judgment

How people interpret and rely on AI systems in media and everyday decision-making (trust, persuasion, social desirability, algorithmic cues).

Methods: experiments, surveys, causal inference.

Computational Social Science

Large-scale text analysis of news and social media to measure narratives, attention cycles, and event-driven dynamics.

Methods: NLP, topic modeling, time-series, ITS.

Science & Climate Communication

Media coverage patterns, public engagement, and how technology can personalize climate information without sacrificing accuracy.

Methods: content analysis, computational measurement, mixed methods.
